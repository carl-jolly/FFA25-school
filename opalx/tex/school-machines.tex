\section{Coordinates and absolute placement}
The ring lies in the global $X$--$Z$ plane, with $Y$ vertical. The launch origin
lies near the beam, whose initial forward direction is $+Z$. The reference
circle has centre $\vect C=(-R_0,0,0)$:
\begin{equation}
 \vect R(\varphi)=\begin{pmatrix}R_0(\cos\varphi-1)\\0\\R_0\sin\varphi\end{pmatrix},
 \quad s=R_0\varphi,\quad
 \vect t(\varphi)=\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\varphi\\0\\\cos\varphi\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:circle}
\end{equation}
Here $s$ is reference arc length and $\vect t$ the unit tangent. A magnet with
entrance azimuth $\alpha_i$ uses the explicit OPALX pose
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 X_i&=R_0(\cos\alpha_i-1),&Y_i&=0,&Z_i&=R_0\sin\alpha_i,\\
 \texttt{THETA}_i&=-\alpha_i,&\texttt{PHI}_i&=0,&\texttt{PSI}_i&=0.
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:pose}
\end{equation}
The position denotes the \emph{entrance}, not the body centre. Local $x$ points
radially outwards and local $z$ follows the entrance tangent. The transformation
from local coordinates to the laboratory is
\begin{equation}
 \vect r=\vect R(\alpha_i)+Q_i\vect r_i,\qquad
 Q_i=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\alpha_i&0&-\sin\alpha_i\\0&1&0\\
 \sin\alpha_i&0&\cos\alpha_i\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:transform}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $Q_i(0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}=\vect t(\alpha_i)$ and
$\vect R(\alpha_i)+Q_i(-R_0,0,0)^{\mathsf T}=\vect C$.
Every element therefore shares the same curvature centre. A body of arc length
$\ell_i$ exits at $\vect R(\alpha_i+\ell_i/R_0)$.

The fringe-model centre is halfway along the nominal body:
\begin{equation}
 \varphi_{c,i}=\alpha_i+\frac{\ell_i}{2R_0}.
 \label{eq:centre}
\end{equation}

\section{Spiral faces and fringe fields}
For positive $R_0$, the common cylindrical and spiral coordinates are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 r&=\sqrt{(X+R_0)^2+Z^2},\qquad
 \varphi=\operatorname{atan2}(Z,X+R_0),\\
 \chi_i&=\varphi-\varphi_{c,i}-\tan\delta\ln(r/R_0).
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:spiral}
\end{equation}
The azimuth uses the branch local to the element. Constant $\chi_i$ describes a
logarithmic spiral. In particular, the nominal body faces satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \varphi_{i,\rm in}(r)&=\alpha_i+\tan\delta\ln(r/R_0),\\
 \varphi_{i,\rm out}(r)&=\alpha_i+\ell_i/R_0+\tan\delta\ln(r/R_0).
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:faces}
\end{equation}
Since $r\,\mathrm{d}\varphi/\mathrm{d}r=\tan\delta$, a face makes the constant
angle $\delta$ with the radial direction. Changing $\delta$ changes the face
shape, while leaving its entrance at $r=R_0$ fixed.

The midplane scaling law separates radial strength and azimuthal fringe shape:
\begin{equation}
 B_Y(r,0,\varphi)=\left(\frac{r}{R_0}\right)^k\sum_i B_{0i}F_i(R_0\chi_i).
 \label{eq:field}
\end{equation}
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
The sum includes elements whose field support contains the evaluation point.
The School uses symmetric parameters within an \texttt{ASYMMETRIC\_ENGE} model:
\begin{equation}
 E(u)=\frac{1}{1+\exp[\sum_{n=0}^{n_a}a_n(u/\lambda)^n]},\qquad
 F_i(s)=E(-s-\ell_i/2)+E(s-\ell_i/2)-1.
 \label{eq:enge}
\end{equation}
Here $\lambda$ is the fringe-length parameter and $\ell_i$ the fringe model's
centre length. Neither is an integrated magnetic length. The nominal faces are
not exact half-field contours for a general Enge polynomial; fields extend
beyond them. The vertical field expansion retains
\texttt{MAX\_Y\_POWER=4}.

The aperture is $R_0-\Delta r_-\leq r\leq R_0+\Delta r_+$,
$|Y|\leq H/2$. The input \texttt{AZIMUTHAL\_EXTENT} is a length $A$; its angular
support is $|\chi_i|\leq A/R_0$. All three machines use
$A=R_0(2\pi/16)$. This window is wider than the body and permits overlapping
fringe contributions in Eq.~\eqref{eq:field}.

\section{Sixteen-cell F/D ring at 12 MeV}
The reference circumference is $2\pi R_0=27.771679$ m. Let
$\Delta=2\pi/16=22.5\degunit$. The sixteen cells have entrance azimuths
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\alpha_{j,F}=6.5425\degunit+j\Delta,\qquad
        \alpha_{j,D}=13.40625\degunit+j\Delta.}
 \label{eq:uniform}
\end{equation}
Here $j=0,\ldots,15$. The centre azimuths are $8.7925\degunit+j\Delta$ and
$14.53125\degunit+j\Delta$. The lengths are $\ell_F=0.3471459882$ m and
$\ell_D=0.1735729941$ m.

The OPALX field coefficients are $B_{0F}=+0.6532097983$ T and
$B_{0D}=-0.5027652364$ T. The input uses $\delta=3.142/4$ rad, rather than exactly
$\pi/4$. Its Enge coefficients are
$(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)=(-1.3,3.42,-1.20,0.23)$ with $\lambda=0.12$ m.
The aperture parameters are $\Delta r_-=0.8$ m, $\Delta r_+=1.2$ m and $H=1$ m.
Zero-field arcs fill the gaps between bodies and close the reference geometry.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$55\degunit$}{55-degree} ring with D magnets off}
The second machine keeps the first ring's radius, energy, field index, Enge
coefficients, apertures, body lengths and absolute entrance poses. It changes
only the spiral angle and D strength:
\begin{equation}
 \delta=55\degunit,\qquad B_{0F}=+0.6532097983\ \mathrm{T},\qquad B_{0D}=0.
\end{equation}
Sixteen F magnets remain active. The sixteen D elements retain their nominal
arcs and apertures but contribute no field. Turning them off is therefore
distinct from deleting their geometry or shifting the F magnets. The larger
spiral angle changes each F face's radial sweep through Eq.~\eqref{eq:faces}.

\section{Fourfold ring at 3 MeV}
The reference circumference is $2\pi R_0=25.132741$ m. Sixteen F/D pairs form
four repetitions of a four-pair superperiod. The pairs differ in both strength
and spacing; the complete ring has fourfold, not sixteenfold, symmetry.
The input's \texttt{N\_CELLS=16} specifies the nominal pair count.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.55\textwidth]{school-fourfold-3MeV-plan.pdf}
\caption{Fourfold ring at 3 MeV. F bodies are blue, D bodies red; zero-field arcs are grey. The dashed circle has radius $R_0$; the black dot marks the global origin.
The horizontal coordinate is translated by $R_0$ to centre the ring.
No particle trajectory is plotted.}
\end{figure}


For $\Delta=2\pi/16$, the angular spacing corrections are
\begin{equation}
 (\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3,\epsilon_4)
       =(-0.0125,-0.125,-0.0125,+0.15).
\end{equation}
These numbers are in \emph{radians}. Each pair occupies the reference arc
$R_0(\Delta+\epsilon_j)$, while its body lengths remain
\begin{equation}
 \ell_F=0.2\Delta R_0=0.3141592654\ \mathrm{m},\qquad
 \ell_D=0.1\Delta R_0=0.1570796327\ \mathrm{m}.
\end{equation}
Define the angular pair starts within a superperiod by
\begin{equation}
 q_1=0,\qquad q_j=\sum_{h=1}^{j-1}(\Delta+\epsilon_h).
\end{equation}
Since $\sum_j\epsilon_j=0$, four pairs span exactly $4\Delta=\pi/2$.
For superperiod $m=0,1,2,3$, set
$\Phi_m=\Phi_0+m\pi/2$ with
$\Phi_0=(0.6\Delta+0.15)/2=0.1928097245$ rad. The entrance angles are
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \alpha_{m,j,F}&=\Phi_m+q_j-0.075\Delta,\\
 \alpha_{m,j,D}&=\Phi_m+q_j+0.2625\Delta.
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:fourfold}
\end{equation}
Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pose} and \eqref{eq:fourfold} specifies every entrance
translation and rotation. The first-superperiod values are listed below.

\section{Native geometry and orbit checks}
The machine figures use vertices stored in native
\texttt{\_ElementPositions.py} files. The footprints are nominal aperture/display
volumes, not mechanical yokes or full fringe-field envelopes.

The companion script decodes the compressed vertices without executing the
plotting code. It matches every mesh to an input pose and checks the spiral
centre and face angles, radial bounds and total arc length. All 65 nominal
elements per ring agree with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pose} and \eqref{eq:faces}; the maximum
angular mismatch is below $2\times10^{-15}$ rad. This verifies the geometry
conversion, not the accuracy of a tracking integrator.

The existing native COF outputs give the values below. At the $Z=0$ section,
$R_{\rm co}=R_0+X_{\rm co}$ and $Y=P_Y=0$. Thus $R_0$ fixes the placement circle,
while the magnetic fields determine a different closed orbit. \\
